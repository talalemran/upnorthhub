The old $10-a-month AI add-on disappeared entirely. And the platform quietly shifted from "a note-taking app with AI features" into something closer to an operating system for autonomous work — agents that read your workspace, execute multi-step tasks, and run in the background on a schedule.

## What Notion Actually Is

At its core, Notion is a flexible workspace built from blocks — text, databases, tables, kanban boards, calendars — that you can combine into pages structured however your work actually needs. That flexibility is both its biggest strength and the source of most new users' early confusion: unlike a dedicated note app or task manager, Notion doesn't tell you how to organize your work. You build the structure yourself.

In 2026, that foundation now sits underneath a genuine AI layer, not just a bolted-on writing assistant. Since September 2025, Notion AI has functioned as an agent platform that can execute multi-step tasks autonomously for up to 20 minutes across hundreds of pages — drafting documents, building databases, searching connected tools like Slack and Google Drive, all from a single prompt rather than a series of manual steps.

## Notion's Pricing Tiers in 2026

Notion runs four tiers, and the AI question is now inseparable from which tier you pick — full AI access is no longer something you can buy separately.

| Plan | Price (annual) | What You Get |
|---|---|---|
| Free | $0 | Unlimited blocks, solo use, limited AI trial |
| Plus | $10/user/month | Team collaboration, unlimited file uploads, 30-day version history, basic AI writing only |
| Business | $20/user/month | Full Notion AI, AI Agents, Ask Notion, Enterprise Search, AI Meeting Notes, private teamspaces |
| Enterprise | Custom | SAML SSO, audit logs, SCIM provisioning, dedicated support |

The critical line separating a genuinely useful AI experience from a limited taste of it sits at Business. Plus includes only basic AI writing assistance — the autonomous Notion Agent, Ask Notion workspace search, and AI Meeting Notes all require Business at minimum. That's a real jump for a solo user or small team, and it's worth being deliberate about whether you actually need those specific features before committing to it.

**Best for solo users and small personal projects:** the Free plan, which includes unlimited blocks and is genuinely usable long-term without ever touching a paid tier.

**Best for small teams needing shared wikis and databases:** Plus, primarily for unlimited file storage, extended version history, and unlimited guests — Notion removed the guest cap across all paid plans in March 2026.

**Best for teams that actually want the AI Agent and workspace search:** Business, since that's the only tier where full AI functionality is genuinely unlocked rather than sampled.

## Understanding Notion AI and Agents

<img src="/images/articles/the-complete-notion-guide-for-2026/02.webp" alt="notion logo in a smartphone" loading="lazy" width="1200" height="800" />

This is the area that's changed most dramatically, and it's worth breaking down carefully because the terminology genuinely matters.

**Notion Agent (personal)** is the AI built into your workspace that you interact with by chatting — it reads your workspace context and executes tasks like building a page, populating a database, drafting a document, or searching connected apps. This comes included with the Business plan and doesn't consume any separate credits.

**Custom Agents** are a different, more advanced tier of automation: autonomous workflows that run in the background, triggered by database changes, schedules, or Slack messages, without you needing to prompt them each time. These launched in February 2026 as a free beta and moved to general availability in May 2026 — and critically, they now run on a separate, metered credit system rather than being included in your Business subscription.

**Notion Credits**, the system powering Custom Agents specifically, cost $10 per 1,000 credits, purchased separately by workspace admins on top of the Business or Enterprise plan. Credits pool across your entire workspace, bill monthly, and reset each cycle with no rollover — meaning unused credits don't carry forward, so overbuying "just in case" wastes budget.

**Best for teams just starting with Notion AI:** stick to the included Notion Agent and basic AI writing tools first — they don't touch the credit system at all, and most teams get real value here before ever needing Custom Agents.

**Best for teams with a genuinely repetitive, well-defined workflow:** Custom Agents, but budget the credit cost deliberately rather than turning them on broadly and discovering the bill later.

## The Model Picker: A Feature Worth Knowing About

One detail that separates Notion's AI approach from most competitors is model flexibility. As of the January 2026 release, users can choose between multiple leading AI models — including options from OpenAI, Anthropic, and Google — directly inside the same chat thread, with an "Auto" option that picks the best model for a given task automatically.

Switching models mid-conversation keeps your context intact — the page, the database, and any connected apps stay in scope even as you swap which model is doing the reasoning. That's a genuinely useful design choice: different models have different strengths, and Notion effectively lets you pick the right one per task rather than locking you into a single provider.

## Practical Ways to Start Using Notion Well

Rather than trying to master every feature on day one, a focused starting sequence works better for both individuals and teams:

1. **Start with one core workflow, not your whole life.** Pick the single area where you waste the most time searching for information or recreating documents — a project tracker, a content calendar, a personal task list — and build that first.
2. **Use a template rather than building from a blank page.** Notion's own template gallery and third-party libraries cover most common use cases (CRMs, content pipelines, personal dashboards) and save significant setup time versus designing a database structure from scratch.
3. **Turn on AI Meeting Notes for a real week of meetings before judging it.** This feature specifically benefits from a full week of realistic use rather than a single test run, since its value compounds as it builds a searchable record over time.
4. **Run one real Notion Agent task on an actual project**, not a toy example — the gap between what the Agent can theoretically do and what it does well on your specific workspace only becomes clear with real use.
5. **Watch the Credits Dashboard for a full month before scaling Custom Agents.** This avoids the common mistake of turning on multiple automated agents at once and being surprised by the resulting credit consumption.

## Where Notion Falls Short

To be fair to anyone evaluating whether to commit to this platform, a few honest limitations belong here.

- **The learning curve is real, especially for teams.** Notion's flexibility means there's no single "correct" way to set it up, which is empowering for power users and genuinely overwhelming for teams without someone driving the structure.
- **Full AI functionality is now Business-tier or higher.** Solo users and small teams on Free or Plus get a meaningfully limited AI experience, and the jump to Business is a real cost increase to consider carefully.
- **Custom Agents' credit system adds a genuine budgeting variable.** Unlike a flat per-seat AI subscription, credit-metered automation means costs can scale unpredictably if agents aren't monitored.
- **Some agent features are still maturing.** Notion's own team has described autonomous task execution as still evolving — treat it as a genuinely useful but imperfect tool, not a fully autonomous replacement for manual work yet.

## The Bottom Line

Notion in 2026 is a meaningfully different product than it was even two years ago — the AI layer has moved from a writing assistant bolted onto a note-taking tool into a genuine agent platform capable of autonomous, multi-step execution. Whether that's worth the Business-tier price jump depends entirely on whether your workflow actually benefits from workspace-wide AI search, meeting transcription, and agent automation, or whether the Free or Plus tier's core organizational flexibility already covers what you need. Start with one real workflow, use the included Notion Agent before paying for Custom Agent credits, and scale up only once you've proven the value on something real.

## FAQ

**Is Notion AI included in the free plan?**

Only a limited trial — full AI functionality, including the Notion Agent and Ask Notion workspace search, requires the Business plan at $20 per user per month billed annually.

**What's the difference between the Notion Agent and Custom Agents?**

The Notion Agent is the personal, chat-based AI included with Business that executes tasks when you prompt it; Custom Agents are autonomous, credit-metered workflows that run in the background on triggers or schedules without needing a prompt each time.

**How much do Notion Custom Agents actually cost?**

Credits cost $10 per 1,000, purchased separately by workspace admins on top of the Business plan, pooled across the workspace, and reset monthly with no rollover for unused credits.

**Can I still get the old $10-a-month Notion AI add-on?**

No — Notion retired the standalone AI add-on for new customers in 2025, and full AI access is now bundled exclusively into the Business and Enterprise plans.

**Is Notion worth paying for as a solo user?**

The Free plan is genuinely usable long-term for solo work with unlimited blocks, so it's worth upgrading only if you specifically need the full AI Agent, unlimited file uploads, or extended version history that the paid tiers unlock.
